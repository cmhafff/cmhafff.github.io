The Origin Story

As a forever lover of makeup, I often found myself scrolling through TikTok and seeing cosmetics brands being criticized for not being inclusive enough. That made me wonder whether data and optimization could help brands approach inclusivity in a more practical way.

For my master’s thesis, I developed an optimization framework to support more inclusive product decisions in the cosmetics industry. The project caught the attention of my program director, who praised its creative application of Operations Research to the beauty industry and agreed to serve as my second reader.

“Can Operations Research help beauty brands become more inclusive?”

The Business Problem

Cosmetics brands are increasingly expected to serve a wider range of skin tones, yet simply adding more products is not always financially or operationally realistic. The real challenge is not to offer as many shades as possible, but to identify which shades should be prioritized for each market. Brands must balance customer needs, local market characteristics, product coverage, development costs, and limited resources.

How can a beauty brand improve inclusivity without continuously increasing its R&D budget?

From Limited Data to an Optimization Framework

STEP 01

Simulate the data

Since no suitable public dataset was available, I simulated the data through statistical analysis while incorporating the qualitative characteristics of each market.

STEP 02

Measure inclusivity

I developed an inclusivity metric based on the Gini coefficient to evaluate how evenly different customer groups were covered by the product portfolio.

STEP 03

Optimize the portfolio

I developed a Mixed-Integer Convex Quadratic Programming model to maximize market coverage and the inclusivity score while keeping total R&D costs within budget.

More Shades Do Not Always Mean More Inclusivity

The analysis showed that offering more shades does not automatically lead to better inclusivity. What matters is selecting the right shades for each market based on local demand and product characteristics. A targeted product strategy can provide better customer coverage without requiring brands to continuously expand their portfolio.
